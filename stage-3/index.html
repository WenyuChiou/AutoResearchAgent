Why plan before running?

  • A promising direction still leaves work to decide. Researchers need to know what evidence to gather, what to compare and when to stop.
  • Stage 3 turns the choice into a study plan. We trace the handoff, follow one household case, then compare the quality of the plans.

Stage 2 recap

  • Compare candidate directions. Bring prior work, evidence and proposals into one view.
  • Review independently. Reconcile disagreements and record the conditions attached to each direction.
  • Hand off the selected direction. Carry forward its supporting evidence and unresolved questions.

Stage 3 design

Where native Codex can fall short

  • It can name a dataset without checking what is in it. The harness checks sources, units and missing fields, then searches for evidence on the gaps that matter.
  • It can say “run the experiment” and skip what to hold fixed. The harness links each question to data, a baseline and steps, then checks the required plan fields.
  • It can propose an analysis without saying how to judge the result. A separate design review checks whether the plan can answer the question and be carried out. Findings guide revision or a narrower study.

Case · LLM households and spending

  • Can households represented by a language model reproduce human spending? If the decision rule passes validation, compare demand and prices under two age mixes.

P1 Research roadmap

  • Check spending first. Compare LLM household predictions with held-out human spending.
  • Check price response separately. Use matched prices and quantities before enabling feedback.
  • Keep the comparisons distinct. Compare decision rules on the same inputs. In the market scenarios, change only the age mix.

What does a one-point shift change?

  2. Multiply the difference by 0.01.
  3. The weighted annual mean changes by -373.25 USD per consumer unit.

P2 Study workflow

  • Reproduce historical spending first. Use 2023 U.S. spending diaries to set the simple rule and give the LLM reference examples. Test both on unseen diary weeks from January to December 2024. Each predicts dollars spent on food at home for one consumer unit over one week.
  • Set up S0 and S1. S0 uses the 2023 U.S. age mix. S1 raises the 65+ share from 27.63% to 32.63%. Start both with the same profile pool, weekly budgets, prices, supply and rules. Run 52 simulated weeks per scenario. S1 is a hypothetical age mix with no forecast year.
  • Follow one week in the food market. Give the LLM the unit’s age band, size and income band, its weekly food budget, and the available foods and their current unit prices. It returns how many units it wants of each food. Code checks that requests use listed items and fit the budget. The market allocates supply and updates next week’s prices.
  • Compare the two paths. Run the market only after the spending and price-response checks pass. Compare weekly demand, purchases and prices for S0 and S1. Repeat both runs with prices fixed at their starting values to measure how much feedback changes the gap.

Evaluation · Codex vs. Codex + Harness

  • Compare the plans the two setups produce. Use the same task and model in six pairs. One Codex reviewer scored the plans with the setup visible.
  • Ask whether another researcher can use the plan. The five criteria come from Goel et al. The reviewer adapted the paper’s 1–10 overall scale to each criterion.

Future Work

  • Stabilize Stages 1–3. Trace the research goal from Stage 1 to the selected direction in Stage 2. Verify that Stage 3 saves the complete, versioned plan with its evidence and checks. Test whether interrupted work can resume without losing the selection or evidence.
  • Evaluate on more research cases. Compare complete plans with the same rubric and balanced presentation order. Add independent reviewers and track which criteria improve.
  • Develop Stages 4–6. Prototype the later stages after the first three are stable. Define what each stage receives, what it delivers, and how researchers verify the result.
