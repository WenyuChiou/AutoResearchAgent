# Stage 1 topic-core literature standard (general v1)

This is a public **production** standard. It applies to any research topic; a
case-specific rubric or private evaluation answer is not part of it. Use
`schemas/core-assessment.v1.schema.json` and the `stage1_core.assessment`
validator for source-bound nominations. Passing that validator proves that
the nominated passages exist in the bound source versions, not that the
scientific interpretation is correct.

- **Topic-core** means that a work makes a material, source-supported
  contribution to a requirement of the current question. Record its actual
  contribution, the decision it affects, what omission would change, and
  whether another work could substitute. Publication age and citation count
  alone do not decide this tier.
- **Classic-supported** means historical recognition or durable uptake is
  supported by two independent recognition sources. This status is separate
  from topic-core. A new closest method can be topic-core without being classic;
  an old famous work can be outside this question's core.
- **Closest-supported** means a same-version source supports substantive
  comparison on the question, system, method and outcome. A candidate may be
  new and may have no citation history. A claimed absence of closer work is
  bounded by the actual search paths, cutoff and access limits.

For each retained work, keep topic requirement IDs, literature roles, selection
tier, historical and closest status, the source version, exact quoted passages,
limitations, and alternatives considered. A source's own text can support its
method or result. It cannot by itself prove that the field regards it as a
classic. Source identity, claim support, topical importance and search
completeness remain separate judgments.

The operational coverage gate still checks saved search execution and reviewed
cluster records. It does not turn a topic-core nomination or a passing
checkpoint into a scientific P2 score. Independent evaluation uses the same
standard but records its own verdict without overwriting the producer's claim.
